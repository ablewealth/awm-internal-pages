💡 Strategic Guidance

  • Bracket Straddling: Allocate just enough to Pre-Tax to drop your current marginal rate (e.g., from 37% to 35%). Push the remainder into Roth.
  • Tax Diversification: Even if 100% Roth is optimal, a blend (e.g., 70/30) is safer. Some Pre-Tax money lets you fill the lowest brackets (10% & 12%) in retirement almost tax-free.
  • Medicare IRMAA Trap: If retirement income is already very high, lean heavily toward Roth. Large Pre-Tax balances force massive RMDs at age 75, triggering Medicare IRMAA penalties.

📄 Methodology & Mathematical Breakdown ▲

1. Annual Loop & Catch-Ups

The engine runs a year-by-year compounding simulation. It dynamically applies standard age 50+ catch-ups and the SECURE 2.0 "Super Catch-Up" limits for ages 60–63, inflating these limits annually if toggled.

2. Bracket Straddling (The Tax Engine)

To calculate exact tax savings, the engine maps current income against progressive federal and state brackets. It calculates total tax owed with and without the contribution to find the true, blended effective savings rate of that specific dollar amount.

3. The Side-Fund (Tax Drag)

If "Reinvest Tax Savings" is active, the engine deposits the exact dollar amount of your tax savings into a taxable brokerage account each year. While the 401(k) grows at the full expected return, this side-fund grows at a reduced rate (Return minus Tax Drag) to simulate annual taxation of dividends and realized gains.

4. Liquidation Phase

At retirement, the entire Pre-Tax 401(k) balance is taxed at your projected Future Marginal Rate. For the Side-Fund, the engine separates your basis from the market growth, and taxes only the growth at LTCG rates plus the 3.8% NIIT.
